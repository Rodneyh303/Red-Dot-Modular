# Multi-group conservation & correlation -- the principle goes scale-invariant

Rodney's insight: with multiple Intertropical + Lantern + Changi GROUPS, the conservation law that
started as poly VERTICAL extends much further into the HORIZONTAL. Working it through, the system's
organising principle (conservation + correlation) turns out to be SCALE-INVARIANT -- it operates
identically on notes and on whole arrangements. And the correlation comes from the SAME place it always
did: Change Alley (plus clocking for the timing half). Pitch-ready + a design requirement for the
grouping system.

## Vertical -> horizontal -> multi-group (three stages of one principle)
1. VERTICAL (one instant): the poly budget -- N voices distributed across slots/outputs NOW; Shophouse
   conserves harmonically down the simultaneous stack. A column, conserved per time-slice.
2. HORIZONTAL (over time, one group): Intertropical scenes make the allocation EVOLVE -- voice
   substitution, seating rotating scene by scene. The vertical budget becomes a horizontal arrangement:
   same slots, refilled across the scene timeline. Conservation now spans the scene sequence.
3. MULTI-GROUP (parallel arrangements): multiple Intertropicals, each its own scene timeline / slot
   budget / output group / Lantern / Changi, all drawing from the SAME poly source. The budget stops
   being a column and becomes a PLANE: vertical (voices in a group at an instant) x horizontal (scenes
   over time) x across-groups (parallel arrangements of one source). A 2D allocation FIELD.

## Polyphony of arrangements (the musical payoff)
Multiple Intertropicals from one source = ONE generative source ARRANGED SEVERAL WAYS SIMULTANEOUSLY --
not copies of the sequencer. E.g. Group 1 = slow harmonic pad (long scenes, chord-tone substitution),
Group 2 = fast rhythmic hocket (short scenes, alternating seating), Group 3 = bassline (one slot, own
transpose) -- all three from the same engine, each conserving its own budget on its own timeline, each
visualised (Lantern) and jacked out (Changi). Contrapuntal arrangement of a single generative source.

## SCALE-INVARIANCE: the correlation continuum recurses one level up
The correlation continuum (homophony <-> heterophony <-> independent polyphony) we described for the
poly VOICES operates IDENTICALLY on the multi-group ARRANGEMENTS:
- groups tightly correlated (same scenes, same seating) = HOMOPHONIC arrangement (move together)
- groups loosely correlated (shared source, different timing/seating) = HETEROPHONIC arrangement (one
  material, ornamented several ways at the ARRANGEMENT level)
- groups independent = POLYPHONIC arrangement (independent voices-of-arrangement)
Same idea, one scale up. The organising principle is scale-invariant across notes and arrangements.

## WHERE the correlation comes from (the mechanism -- must be real, not metaphor)
It's the SAME engine, reaching the arrangement level through voice selection -- NOT a re-implementation:

- CONTENT correlation across groups = CHANGE ALLEY. The groups draw from the same poly voices, which
  are ALREADY correlated by Change Alley's matrix before any Intertropical sees them. When Group 1
  seats voice 3 and Group 2 seats voice 5, the relationship between what the two groups PLAY is
  inherited from the Change-Alley correlation between voices 3 and 5. Pinned to the same source ->
  the groups play correlated material though they're independent arrangers; decorrelated -> independent.
  So inter-group content correlation = the Change Alley correlation between the seated voices. One
  correlation engine, expressed at two scales -> the scale-invariance is LITERAL (same matrix), not
  analogy.

- TIMING correlation across groups = NOT Change Alley (honest bound). Change Alley correlates WHAT the
  voices play, not the SCENE TIMELINES of different Intertropicals. Whether Group 1's scenes advance in
  step with Group 2's is a function of CLOCKING -- shared clock / phase relationship / polymetric
  offset (phase drive, Sands/IT polymeter). A separate axis.

- THE FULL PICTURE = a PRODUCT of the two:
  * correlated content + correlated timing  = homophonic arrangement (move together, related material)
  * correlated content + independent timing = HETEROPHONIC arrangement (shared material, different
    rhythmic strata) -- note: this is EXACTLY gamelan layering (one melodic core, multiple time
    strata). The East/Singapore thread reappears at the arrangement scale, unforced.
  * decorrelated both = independent polyphony of arrangements.
So: Change Alley does the CONTENT half, clocking/phase does the TIMING half, and they compose. Richer
and more honest than "Change Alley does it" -- and every claim is mechanistically backed.

## DESIGN CONSEQUENCE for the grouping/pairing system (deferred, but this raises the bar)
The Lantern/T3 pairing system was scoped as "which visualiser/breakout binds to which Intertropical."
This insight makes it MORE: if inter-group correlation is to be first-class, the grouping substrate
should also make it easy to (a) point multiple groups at the SAME source, and (b) relate their CLOCKING
(shared/phase/polymetric) so the timing half of the correlation is controllable, not accidental.
Pairing = identity binding (Lantern/T3 <-> IT); GROUPING = source-sharing + clock-relating across ITs.
Build pairing first (needed for one pair), but design it knowing grouping/correlation-across-groups is
the larger target so the data model doesn't preclude it.

## Pitch line
The system's organising principle -- conserve a budget, correlate the parts -- is SCALE-INVARIANT: it
works on notes (poly voices, via Change Alley) and identically on whole ARRANGEMENTS (multiple
Intertropical+Lantern+Changi groups sharing one source). Correlated content (Change Alley) x correlated
timing (clock/phase) lets you arrange one generative source homophonically, heterophonically or
polyphonically at the ARRANGEMENT level -- polyphony of arrangements. Few if any systems do this.
